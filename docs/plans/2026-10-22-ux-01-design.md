Status: approved by the Director with the answers to its three review points (decision D28, recorded 2026-10-22T06:31:38.935Z, CHG-20261022-004). Proposed 2026-10-22T05:10:11.915Z (CHG-20261022-003). Branch `claude/ux-01-reference-screens`; it follows the [intake](2026-10-22-ux-01-intake.md) and decision D27. The [implementation plan](2026-10-22-ux-01-implementation.md) follows from it; the screens are built (CHG-20261022-005 to CHG-20261022-013), and the Director declined their visual approval (D29). The [design audit](../audits/2026-10-23-ux-01-design-audit.md) finds that this design specified components and states but no visual world, and that its compositions follow the legacy app. Implementation adjustments are recorded in the design-system validation and ADR 0017. Where a passage below still names a review point, the [Director's review](#directors-review-decision-d28) section gives the answer.

## Agreed scope

- **One matched set (D27):** four populated reference screens in the adopted standard. They are the crowded match, the practical deckbuilder, the Collection binder and pack presentation, each with an interaction specification (UX standard §36). The Director's approval of the set is the reference-direction selection.
- **Medium (D27):** interactive compositions in the design system's Storybook. They are built from the existing components plus the new pieces listed below, on real Ravensburger catalog cards (D25). Every screen carries a visible demonstration label. `apps/web` receives no demonstration data.
- **Match scene (D27):** the crowded match includes a first static Babylon table scene beneath its interface panels. The Flat tier and the semantic board stay complete (§19, §30).
- **Ink forms (D27):** quiet, documented slots at room perimeters, with no artwork.
- **Not in scope:**
  - the enhanced card inspector (D24, UX-03); the screens use the existing Experimental `CardInspector`;
  - CardFX;
  - live play, engine use, economy and ownership services;
  - catalog delivery to the app;
  - device, browser and performance qualification;
  - Stable component status.

Every screen follows the brief contract of §33: the player task, priority order, approved reference, representative content, required states, responsive behavior and acceptance evidence. The approved reference is the adopted standard plus the component design approved as shown (D26).

## Shared foundations

- **Room frame.**
  - Collection, Decks and Packs share one navigation bar: the wordmark, the primary destinations of blueprint §10.1 (Play, Collection, Decks, Lab, Learn, Events, Profile), the quiet notification count and the profile menu.
  - The match replaces it with focused match chrome. A menu in that chrome holds settings, rules and legal information, and concede.
  - The fan-content notice stays reachable from every screen through the menu. It also appears in the required loading context (§29).
- **Demonstration label.** A short, persistent label on every screen: "Demonstration: real cards; position, ownership and contents are invented." It uses the metadata text role and sits in the room header, never over a card. Its docs contract states why it exists (§24, §33).
- **Ink-form slots.** An `InkFormSlot` region marks each perimeter area where §4 artwork belongs: the outer gutters of wide rooms (80rem and above) and the match surround. It renders nothing in the canvas tone by default. A Storybook toolbar switch, "Show artwork slots", outlines each slot with its name and size so reviewers can see the reservation. Slots never overlap controls or card information, and they collapse below 80rem.
- **Format values from data.** Lore to win, deck size, copy limit and ink limit come from a fixture format record labeled "Core (demonstration policy)". No component hard-codes them (§19).
- **Text and locale.** All strings go through the existing message catalog with the `en-XA` pseudolocale. Long names come from real cards: the fixture's longest card name and subtitle, and the longest rules text. Player names are invented and visibly demonstration (§28).
- **Viewports.** Desktop 1440 × 900, tablet landscape 1180 × 820 and tablet portrait 820 × 1180, each in Paper and Ink, at comfortable density, with compact density where §11 allows. Phone journeys stay staged in UX-02.

## Screen 1: crowded match

- **Player task.** Understand the position and make the next decision.
- **Priority order:**
  1. the required decision and its context;
  2. one's own available actions;
  3. both players' lore and resources;
  4. the battlefield's objects and states;
  5. the hand;
  6. the event log;
  7. everything else.

**Composition, desktop.**
- **Scene.** The Babylon scene fills the room behind the interface. Panels sit on opaque surfaces (§12), and the battlefield region in the centre shows the scene.
- **Top strip.** The opponent's public identity: neutral initials avatar, name, lore against the format's threshold, hand count, deck count, discard and ready or exerted ink. Then the connection status, the quiet notification count and the match menu.
- **Battlefield.** The opponent's side above, the player's below. Each side has a front row of characters and a back row of items. Locations sit at the outer edge with the characters moved to them grouped beside them.
- **Right column.**
  - **Width:** 22.5rem.
  - **Header:** turn and phase context, for example "Your turn · Turn 7 · Main phase".
  - **Action and choice panel:** always visible while a decision is open (§20).
  - **Below the panel:** a tabbed region switching between the event log and the inspector. Opening inspection selects the inspector tab and closing returns to the log, so the battlefield never moves.
- **Bottom strip.**
  - **Left:** the player's identity, lore, ready ink against total, deck and discard.
  - **Centre:** the hand as a fan, with a Fan/List switch.
  - **Right:** "Quest with all characters" and "End turn". Each keeps its default-on review (§20).
- **Card states.** Card state never depends on rotation or colour alone:
  - exerted (rotated, plus an "Exerted" marker with an icon and text);
  - damage (a counter showing the number and the word);
  - drying, for characters played this turn;
  - Shift stacks, with a count and an "On <card>" line;
  - cards placed under another card;
  - "At <location>";
  - current modifiers as short chips, such as "+2 strength" or "Evasive (gained)".

**Composition, tablet.**
- **Landscape (1180 px, under 80rem).** The log leaves the right column for a "Log" sheet. The right column, now 20rem, keeps turn context, the choice panel and the inspector tab.
- **Portrait (820 px).**
  - The battlefield takes the full width on top.
  - Below it come the choice panel, the hand and the primary actions.
  - Log and inspection open as modal sheets (`InspectorHost` already switches below 64rem).
  - The battlefield keeps its two-dimensional arrangement, and the surrounding panels reflow (§19).

**Representative content.** Two Core-legal decks of two inks each, drawn mainly from Hyperia City (set 14) so that the binder and pack reuse their images. The position is turn 7 of a plausible game:
- **Player:** 12 lore and 7 ink, 5 of them ready. Five characters, including one exerted, one drying, one damaged and one Shift stack; two items; one location with two characters at it; a hand of 6.
- **Opponent:** 14 lore and 7 ink. Six characters, including two exerted and one with a gained keyword; one item; one location; a hand of 7.

Each printing in the position is chosen for a state it exercises, recorded like the existing fixture's selection reasons. The position is authored, not engine-run. Its reachability is not certified, and the demonstration label says so.

**States.** Each state is a story:

| State | Shows |
|---|---|
| Idle, own main phase | The full crowded position |
| Selected | A character selected, with its action menu: Quest, Challenge, Move, Inspect |
| Choosing targets | Valid challenge targets marked; invalid ones carry a reason; local cancel |
| Choice and payment | A song's singer choice, and an ability's "choose one" |
| Review | Bulk-quest review listing the characters; end-turn review with End turn and Keep playing |
| Pending | The submitted command stays identifiable; duplicate submission is blocked; controls carry a reason |
| Rejected | The authoritative reason and the recovery |
| Opponent's turn | Waiting state, the accepted-action log summary and the quiet count |
| Reconnecting and resynchronizing | An unresolved command receipt reconciled, not repeated (§21) |
| Large and small hand | 12 cards and 1 card, with the list alternative |
| Long names | Long player names and the longest real card name |
| Flat tier | Scene off; the same layout in DOM |
| Renderer failure | The scene fails to start and the Flat tier takes over with an explanation |
| Takedown fallback | `officialAssets` off; neutral placeholders keep every name, state and action |

**Acceptance evidence.**
- Every state renders in both themes at all three viewports.
- The semantic board exposes every zone, object, state and available action by keyboard and to screen readers.
- The live region announces only the required choice, accepted-action summaries, connection problems and terminal results (§25).
- The scene and the DOM read the same position object.

## Screen 2: practical deckbuilder

- **Player task.** Build and revise a legal deck without losing work.
- **Priority order:**
  1. the deck's contents and count against the format;
  2. validation near Save;
  3. catalog search and filters;
  4. summary figures;
  5. import and export.

**Composition, desktop.**
- **Header.** The room header names the deck, shows its save state ("Unsaved changes" or "Saved · revision 7") and holds Import, Export and Save.
- **Catalog region (flexible width).**
  - a search field;
  - filters for ink, cost, type, rarity, set, inkable and classification;
  - a Grid/List switch and the result count;
  - the card grid.

  Each grid tile is the D26 card tile: pressing the card opens it for reading. Under it sits one simple control row, `−  2  +`: Remove, the quantity in the deck and Add (D28).
- **Deck region (25rem).** The deck name, format and count ("52 / 60"), its ink identities and Contents/Summary tabs.
  - **Contents:** `DeckEntry` rows grouped by cost.
  - **Summary:** a cost curve with values and a table alternative, the inkable count and the type split. Each figure supports a deck decision (§18).
  - **Validation summary:** pinned at the bottom of the region next to Save. It has three labeled sections:
    - rules for the format and policy version;
    - ownership;
    - advice.

    An ownership limit never reads as a rules error.

**Composition, tablet.** Landscape keeps both regions, with the deck region at 20rem. Portrait becomes one focused pane with Catalog/Deck tabs. A persistent deck summary bar (count, validity and Save) stays in both tabs, and filters open in a sheet (blueprint §11.4).

**Representative content.**
- **Deck:** the player's 60-card match deck, with real card names and printings.
- **Catalog:** limited to the reference fixture, about 80 printings, with an honest result count.
- **Ownership:** demonstration figures, labeled.

**States:**

| State | Shows |
|---|---|
| Working | 52 of 60, valid so far |
| Invalid | Five copies of one card, a card outside the format and a third ink, each naming the card, quantity, reason and policy version |
| Ownership limits | Owned quantities against deck quantities, as warnings distinct from rules |
| Unsaved and leaving | Leaving with unsaved changes: Keep editing, Discard changes, Save |
| Save conflict | The revision changed elsewhere; the local draft is kept and resolution is explicit |
| Import preview | Unresolved names and counts reported before anything changes |
| Export | An explicit format choice |
| Filtered empty | Filters kept, with Reset filters |
| Inspection | Opened from the catalog; closing returns to the same tile with the search kept |
| Loading | The catalog skeleton keeps the known structure |
| Long names | Wrapping card names with full names available |

**Acceptance evidence.**
- Add and remove work by pointer, touch and keyboard, with no dragging required.
- Validation text names the card, quantity, reason and policy.
- Search and filters never discard edits.
- Focus returns to the originating tile after inspection.

## Screen 3: Collection binder

- **Player task.** Browse a set's printings, see what is owned and missing, and inspect a printing.
- **Priority order:**
  1. the binder pages;
  2. the set and position within it;
  3. view, search and filter controls;
  4. completion figures;
  5. inspection.

**Composition, desktop.**
- **Toolbar.** Below the room header: a set picker ("Hyperia City · Set 14"), the Binder/Grid/List view switch, search and filters (owned, missing, foil, ink, rarity).
- **Completion summary.** Set completion uses the set's standard printings as its denominator, for example "112 of 204 printings owned". Special printings are counted separately. Foil availability and foil ownership are separate figures (§17).
- **Spread.** Two faces of 3 × 3 pockets. The preferred pocket width is 144 px, with 12 px gaps and 16 px face padding, fitted to the available space with a minimum usable pocket size. When inspection is open, pockets shrink or the spread becomes a single face, by content fit.
- **Navigation.** Previous and next page buttons flank the spread, with a page indicator ("Pages 12–13 of 24") and a "Go to card number" field. Page keys are scoped to the focused binder (§17).
- **Inspection.** Selecting a pocket opens the inspector side panel.

**Pocket states:**
- **Owned:** the card, with normal and foil quantities as text and icons.
- **Missing:** the card's silhouette, name and number in a muted treatment. This is the Director's "empty spaces showing what's missing".
- **Ownership unknown** and **ownership unavailable:** labeled; unavailable never shows zero.
- **Loading:** a skeleton pocket.
- **Padding:** blank and not focusable.
- **Section dividers:** each ink section keeps its name and symbol, also without artwork.

**Composition, tablet.** Landscape keeps the spread with smaller pockets. Portrait shows a single face, navigated with the same buttons. Grid and List views keep the search, filters and selected printing (§17).

**Representative content.**
- **Set:** Hyperia City, shown in collector order with its ink sections.
- **Spreads:** two spreads in different sections, with images. Pages outside the fixture's images show their pockets as loading in the loading story only, never as missing.
- **Ownership:** demonstration figures, labeled.

**States:**

| State | Shows |
|---|---|
| Partially owned spread | Mixed normal and foil holdings |
| New player | A genuine empty state with a next action |
| Ownership unavailable | Cards shown; counts marked unavailable |
| Loading | Skeleton pockets |
| Filtered | Owned only, with completion figures unchanged |
| Printing navigation | "Go to 142" anchors the page on that printing and keeps the anchor through a resize |
| Single face | Tablet portrait |
| Grid and List views | Search, filters and selection kept |
| Reduced motion | Page change without spatial travel |
| Takedown fallback | Neutral placeholders keep order and section identity |

**Acceptance evidence.**
- Paging works by buttons and scoped keys, with no swipe required.
- Offscreen pages are out of the focus order.
- Reading and focus order run across the spread.
- Completion figures use the policy denominator.

## Screen 4: pack presentation

- **Player task.** See the contents of a committed opening, at the pace the player chooses.
- **Priority order:**
  1. the revealed cards and their rarity;
  2. the reveal controls;
  3. the summary;
  4. what to do next.

**Composition and sequence.** The flow follows §23: choose → review → submit → resolve receipt → present → summary → return. The product step shows a Hyperia City booster with a quiet artwork slot in place of pack art. Opening products is free, and the copy never implies a purchase (§23).
- **Review.** One concrete sentence: "Open 1 Hyperia City booster? The contents are decided when you open it and saved to your collection."
- **Pending.** An indeterminate state with no invented percentage.
- **Reveal.** 12 face-down cards in two rows of six on desktop. The ceremonial default reveals one card at a time, building to the rarest (the Director's brainstorm answer).
  - **Controls:** "Reveal next" (primary, Enter or Space), "Reveal all" and "Skip to summary".
  - **Each card:** its rarity as text, a "Foil" marker where it applies, and "New" when it is the first copy. The verified symbol set (D21) has no rarity symbols, so none is drawn.
  - **Rarest beat:** restrained light in the surroundings, never over the card, within 900 ms (§26).
- **Summary.** All 12 cards with their labels, the number of new cards, "View in Collection" for each card and for the whole opening, and Done.

**Composition, tablet.** Landscape keeps two rows of six with smaller cards. Portrait uses three rows of four. The controls stay in a fixed bar within thumb reach.

**Representative content.** Twelve Hyperia City printings, taken where possible from the binder's printings:
- six commons;
- three uncommons;
- two of rare or higher;
- one foil.

This is a demonstration composition, not the official distribution, and it says so.

**States:** product choice and review; pending; mid-reveal (5 of 12); the rarest beat; summary; interrupted and resumed at card 6; presentation failure with the contents intact as a text list; settlement pending (contents committed, collection update pending); reduced motion (direct reveal); tablet portrait.

**Acceptance evidence.**
- No control waits on decoration.
- Skip and Reveal all always work.
- Reduced motion shows the same contents.
- The rarity label never depends on effects.
- Resume shows the same contents in the same order.

## Interaction specification

Each machine is documented in the screen's story and enforced by its browser test.

- **Match command.** idle → select or inspect → choose action → choose payment and targets → review when required → submit → pending → accepted, rejected or reconcile → next valid state (§20).
  - **Before submission:** Escape or Cancel steps back one stage, and every selection can be changed.
  - **After submission:** Escape never implies cancellation.
  - **Pending:** blocks duplicates and keeps the originating intent visible.
  - **Reconcile:** resolves an unresolved receipt from the authoritative state. The reference simulates the receipt outcomes with fixture states and never fakes engine results.
- **Bulk quest and end turn.** These are independent reviews, both on by default. Turning one off leaves the other on, and neither affects individual quests (§20).
- **Hand.**
  - **Fan:** arrow keys move focus between cards; Enter selects; I inspects.
  - **List:** the same actions as rows.
  - Hover or focus raises a preview above the fan without moving the card's hit area (§4, §14).
- **Deck editing.** Add, remove or set a quantity: local edit → unsaved → save → saved or conflict. Undo covers local edits. Leaving with unsaved changes asks first (§18).
- **Binder paging.** Next and previous step one spread or face. "Go to card number" anchors to that printing. A resize or view change keeps the anchor. In reduced motion the page changes by direct replacement or a brief fade (§17, §26).
- **Pack reveal.** pending → ready → revealing(n) → summary, with resume at n after an interruption. Skip and Reveal all go straight to summary, and the contents never change (§23).
- **Keyboard shortcuts.** Shortcuts are listed in each screen's help menu. Single-character shortcuts (I for inspect) are off by default and can be switched on. They never fire while a text field has focus (§14).
- **Focus and announcements.** Closing any overlay restores focus to its originating control or the nearest surviving location. Announcements are polite, except connection loss during a decision, which is assertive (§25).

## Babylon table scene

- **Ownership.** The scene lives in `packages/presentation` (§32), as `mountTableScene(canvas, input, options)`. It returns `update(input)`, `cardRects()` and `dispose()`, following the existing `mountSanityScene` pattern of injectable engine creation and resize observation.
  - The design system's components never import it.
  - The match reference story loads it with a dynamic import, through a development-only workspace dependency of the design-system package, the same way the stories use `@lorcana/rules-data`.
  - The import-boundary rules already allow this: both packages are browser scope.
- **What it renders:**
  - an abstract ink-and-paper table in the active theme's renderer colours (`renderer.ts`, linear values, never converted twice);
  - zone surfaces as slightly raised paper planes;
  - one key light and a soft fill, with contact shadows under cards for the 2.5D depth of the brainstorm answers;
  - card meshes with rounded corners, textured from the real thumbnails;
  - exerted cards rotated;
  - Shift stacks offset;
  - face-down cards (inkwell, opponent's hand, decks) showing the official card back (D28);
  - the selection and valid-target states as crisp outline rings in the focus colour, never a glow (§33).

  The camera is fixed, tilted about 20–25 degrees from top-down so cards stay readable and the player's side is nearer. There is no orbit, parallax or shake (§26).
- **Update model.** The scene renders on demand: once after mount, after each `update` and after each resize. It runs no continuous loop and plays no animation in UX-01. React never re-renders it per frame, because it is mounted once and updated imperatively (§30, blueprint §11.3).
- **Interaction and accessibility.**
  - The canvas is `aria-hidden`.
  - The semantic board is a DOM layer of real buttons placed over the scene. Each sits at the screen rectangle `cardRects()` projects from the fixed camera, recomputed on resize. These buttons carry the accessible names, the visible focus ring, pointer and touch hit areas of at least 44 px, and the state markers as text.
  - The scene needs no picking, and keyboard, pointer and screen reader all use one structure that reads the same position as the Flat tier.
- **Failure and fallback.**
  - **Story start:** if the engine cannot be created, textures fail or the context is lost, the story switches to the Flat tier and shows the renderer-failure message (§21).
  - **Takedown:** with `officialAssets` off, card faces use the neutral placeholder.
  - **Unmount:** disposes the engine, textures and observers.
- **Boundary with WEB-01.** UX-01 claims only that the scene shows the reference composition and look. WEB-01 still owns lifecycle across routes and visibility, quality tiers, measured frame times, GPU-context restoration and device behavior. No performance figure is recorded from this scene.
- **Automation limits.** Chromium in CI renders WebGL in software. Automated checks cover the canvas's presence and `aria-hidden`, the overlay buttons' alignment, focus and names, the fallback path (forced engine failure) and axe. Visual comparison of the scene is a human review.

- **Location.** `packages/rules-data` gains a reference fixture beside the existing presentation fixture. `scripts/select-fixtures.mjs` is extended to write it from the dated acquisition, with a reason for every printing. `catalog:verify` checks both fixtures against the dated record (ADR 0018).

- **Selection criteria:**
  - two Core-legal 60-card decks of two inks each, mainly Hyperia City, covering Shift, Singer and a song, a location with a move cost, items, and keyword variety (Evasive, Bodyguard, Ward, Rush, Challenger, Resist);

They are typed with the new components' own props. They are not catalog data and not viewer-contract data; WEB-02 later maps the authorized viewer projection onto the same props.

## Points for the Director's review

1. **Card back.** The scene and the face-down states need a card back. Option A: the official card back, which D07 permits, from a source file with recorded provenance; the Director's asset folder may already hold one. Option B: a neutral back drawn from the design tokens, ink on paper, with no product branding. Recommended: A if a source file exists, otherwise B until one does.
2. **Deckbuilder tile controls.** D26 removed information from under card tiles. The deckbuilder still needs explicit add and remove controls beside each catalog card (§18), and touch has no hover. The design adds one control row under each catalog tile, in the deckbuilder only: the quantity in the deck, Remove and Add. It adds no card information. Recommended: accept, since this is an action row, not the information D26 removed.
3. **Navigation bar.** A top bar across Collection, Decks and Packs, rather than a side rail. It keeps the full width for the binder spread and the deckbuilder's two regions. Recommended: accept.
